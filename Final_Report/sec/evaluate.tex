\subsection{Explainable AI (xAI)}
\label{subsec:xai}

While deep convolutional neural networks achieve state-of-the-art accuracy, their internal decision-making processes often act as opaque black boxes. In early training iterations, we observed significant accuracy variance (from 5\% to over 60\%) under identical hyperparameter configurations without a clear indicator of whether performance gains stemmed from genuine feature representations or empirical memorization. Building upon foundational work in CNN interpretability by Zeiler and Fergus \cite{zeiler2014visualizing}, we employed Gradient-weighted Class Activation Mapping (Grad-CAM) \cite{selvaraju2017grad} to interpret spatial feature attention and verify whether prediction logits are driven by class-discriminative object features or background artifacts.

Grad-CAM extracts the gradients of a target class score $y^c$ with respect to the feature maps $A^k$ of the final convolutional layer:
\begin{equation}
    w_k^c = \frac{1}{Z} \sum_{i} \sum_{j} \frac{\partial y^c}{\partial A_{i,j}^k}
\end{equation}
By computing a coarse localization map as a weighted combination of forward activation maps followed by a Rectified Linear Unit ($\text{ReLU}$), we project class-specific spatial attention back onto the input image space. In our pipeline, activation maps are extracted specifically from the final layer of the backbone (\texttt{model.backbone.features[-1]}), where semantic resolution is highest prior to global average pooling.

\subsubsection{Best-Case Evaluation: Discriminative Feature Localization}
To evaluate spatial attention under optimal model convergence, we analyzed correctly predicted samples from our final fine-tuned model. Figure \ref{fig:cam_bestcase} illustrates a representative best-case scenario for a \texttt{Samoyed} instance, correctly classified with a confidence score of $79.2\%$. 

\begin{figure}[htbp]
    \centering
    \includegraphics[width=\columnwidth]{../CAM_Heat_Map/panel_04040.jpg}
    \caption{Grad-CAM visualization demonstrating strong feature localization for a correct classification. The model concentrates its highest spatial attention (red/orange) directly on the target object (\texttt{Samoyed}), successfully ignoring background distractors such as the handler and fencing.}
    \label{fig:cam_bestcase}
\end{figure}

The overlay map in Figure \ref{fig:cam_bestcase} reveals that peak activation intensity (red and deep orange zones) is strictly localized on the animal's central torso and thick coat structure. Crucially, complex contextual elements—such as the moving legs of the human handler on the left or the wooden fence lattice in the background—receive virtually zero spatial weight (cool blue region). This confirms that the fine-tuned model prioritizes domain-invariant, morphological breed characteristics rather than relying on spatial environment shortcuts.

\subsubsection{Worst-Case Evaluation: Contextual Shortcut Learning}
To contrast this behavior, we conducted xAI analysis on an unregularized baseline model trained without spatial data augmentation. Figure \ref{fig:cam_worstcase} demonstrates a severe failure mode where the baseline network erroneously predicts \texttt{Pug} with a high confidence of $87.66\%$ on a non-Pug target.

\begin{figure}[htbp]
    \centering
    \includegraphics[width=\columnwidth]{../CAM_Heat_Map/panel_03746.jpg}
    \caption{Grad-CAM visualization of a baseline model failure (worst practice). The network exhibits severe shortcut learning, misclassifying the image as \texttt{Pug} with $87.66\%$ confidence by focusing heavily on background contextual features rather than the actual target animal.}
    \label{fig:cam_worstcase}
\end{figure}

As shown by the heatmap in Figure \ref{fig:cam_worstcase}, spatial focus is almost entirely shifted away from the animal subject towards non-relevant background textures and floor boundaries. This high-confidence error illustrates a classical case of \textit{shortcut learning} \cite{geirhos2020shortcut}: instead of learning invariant semantic features of dog breeds, the unregularized backbone memorized spurious correlations between background geometries and the target logit.

\subsubsection{Key xAI Takeaways and Mitigation}
Our xAI diagnostics directly guided our model refinement. Identifying this reliance on background context highlighted the explicit necessity for targeted spatial data augmentation strategies, such as Random Cropping, Color Jitter, and Random Erasing \cite{zhong2020random}. By breaking unwanted correlations between non-relevant background elements and class predictions, these mitigations successfully redirected spatial attention back onto the primary animal targets, leading to improved generalization and robust saliency maps.
