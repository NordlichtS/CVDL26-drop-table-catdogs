\section{Integration Findings}
\label{sec:integration}

Assembling independently developed stages into one system surfaced several
lessons that were as valuable as the headline metrics.

\textbf{The resolution contract.} One cross-stage dependency is input
resolution. Convolutional filters adapt to the object scale seen during
training, so a classifier trained at one crop resolution and evaluated at
another suffers a resolution shift that degrades accuracy without raising any
error. Because different training tracks (Sec.~\ref{sec:training})
experimented with different input sizes, we made the trained resolution part
of each checkpoint's stored metadata, and the inference pipeline resizes the
detector's crop to whatever resolution the loaded classifier was actually
trained at. This turned a recurring source of silent degradation into a
non-issue and decoupled the detector's crop geometry from classifier
retraining decisions.

\textbf{Crop geometry matters.} End-to-end evaluation showed that enlarging
the crop region slightly beyond the raw bounding box measurably improved
breed accuracy: tight boxes occasionally clip ears, whiskers, and coat
texture at the border---precisely the regions fine-grained discrimination
relies on \cite{zhang2014part}. Together with a permissive detection
threshold, this tuning recovered several accuracy points on the labeled
evaluation set; after it, the dominant residual error of the full system was
borderline images yielding no detection at all rather than breed confusion,
indicating that further end-to-end gains lie in the localization stage of
Sec.~\ref{sec:detector} rather than in the classifiers.

\textbf{Editions over rewrites.} Finally, we kept the earlier 20-class
combined model as a maintained ``edition'' alongside the final species-split
system, published as successive notebooks in the repository. Beyond
documenting progress, the combined model served as a permanent integration
fallback and as the reference point for the ablations in
Sec.~\ref{sec:training}; several bugs were caught simply because the two
editions disagreed where they should not have.
